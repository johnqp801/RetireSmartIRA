## WHAT IT IS

RetireSmartIRA answers a question most retirement tools skip: not "will I run out of money," but
"what will I actually owe, and what can I do about it before December 31?"

Retirement tax planning is a set of interlocking decisions that most people make one at a time, in
isolation, usually too late. Convert to a Roth this year or wait. Claim Social Security at 62, at
full retirement age, or at 70. Take the RMD or make it a charitable transfer. Realize a capital
gain now or next year. Each of those decisions moves the others. A Roth conversion raises this
year's income, which can raise your Medicare premium two years from now, reduce an ACA subsidy
today, push more of your Social Security into taxable territory, and trigger the investment income
surtax, all at once.

The app models the whole system at once, for this year and across a full retirement horizon, and
shows the work.

## WHAT IT ACTUALLY DOES

**This year's tax picture.** A single live screen showing federal tax on current-year brackets,
state tax for all 50 states and DC, the net investment income tax, Medicare IRMAA surcharges, ACA
premium credit and cliff risk, standard versus itemized with SALT cap math, and taxable brokerage
income. Change any input and everything updates instantly.

**A scenario builder.** Test Roth conversions, withdrawals, qualified charitable distributions,
cash and appreciated-stock gifts, withholding changes, and Social Security timing before you commit
to any of them.

**A multi-year Roth conversion plan.** Rather than guessing one year at a time, it builds a
conversion ladder across your horizon. You choose the strategy: minimize lifetime tax, fill to a
target bracket, or stay under a Medicare IRMAA tier. It compares all three side by side with each
one's effect on your taxes, your Medicare surcharges, and your heirs. You can override any
individual year, including spending changes and one-time expenses.

**Heir and legacy modeling.** Inherited IRAs are where most calculators quietly get it wrong. This
one handles the 10-year drain rule, spousal inherited IRAs, eligible-beneficiary stretch options,
and the step-up in basis, then shows the trade-off curve between your lifetime taxes and what your
family actually keeps after tax.

**Social Security planning for couples.** Compares claiming ages across both lifetimes, including
survivor outcomes, with a claiming heat map and a couples optimization recommendation. Handles
spousal top-up and the widow penalty, the sharp tax increase a surviving spouse faces when the
household drops from joint to single filing while keeping most of its income.

**RMD calculation.** Your own accounts at 73 or 75 under SECURE 2.0, plus inherited accounts on
their own correct beneficiary schedules.

**Retirement drawdown projection.** Forty years of balance projection under three modes: RMDs only,
a target spending level, or a fixed withdrawal rate, with the point where income crosses the first
IRMAA tier marked.

**Quarterly estimated taxes and safe harbor.** Federal and state safe harbor evaluated
independently, with state-specific rules and Form 2210 underpayment detection.

**Fifty-state comparison.** Model what relocating does to your retirement tax bill.

**A CPA-ready PDF briefing.** Export the whole analysis to hand to a professional.

Behind all of it: twelve calculation engines and roughly 1,900 automated tests that run before
anything ships.

## WHO IT'S FOR

The financially engaged do-it-yourselfer. People who want to understand their numbers rather than be
told what to do. The internal shorthand is "people who actually read IRS Pub 590."

Specifically: pre-retirees mapping a conversion strategy, retirees managing RMDs and IRMAA and the
ACA cliff, couples deciding Social Security timing, federal and military households with FERS or
military retirement or VA disability income, anyone who just inherited an IRA and has ten years to
drain it, and anyone weighing their own tax bill against what they leave behind.

Secondary audience: CPAs and financial advisors who want a second opinion, or a client-ready
briefing they didn't have to build in Excel.

## WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

**It is independent.** Brokerage tools are attached to firms that sell products. This one sells
nothing and recommends no product.

**It goes deep where broad planners go shallow.** Tools like Boldin cover the whole retirement
picture. This covers the tax mechanics underneath it: safe harbor with state-specific rules, IRMAA
thresholds and cliffs, ACA subsidy interaction, state-by-state retirement income exclusions, the
inherited IRA rules that most tools get wrong.

**Nothing is a black box.** Every number traces back to the input that produced it. No proprietary
score, no "our algorithm suggests." If you disagree with a number you can find out exactly where it
came from.

**It is private by construction, not by policy.** There is no account linking and no aggregator. You
type your numbers in and they are computed on your device. There is no server holding your
financial life, because there is no server.

**It is a real native app.** iPhone, iPad, and Mac, not a web page in a wrapper. It works offline.

**It is current.** Every IRS limit, poverty guideline, ACA threshold, and Medicare bracket reflects
current-year values, updated as Congress changes the rules.

## HONEST LIMITATIONS (do not write copy that contradicts these)

- No account linking, aggregation, or auto-import of any kind. The one exception is an optional
  Social Security earnings-history file you can import.
- No Monte Carlo or probability-of-success simulation. Projections are deterministic.
- Voluntary withdrawals from an inherited IRA are not yet modeled in the multi-year projection.
- Tax-exempt municipal bond interest is currently treated as zero in the IRMAA, ACA, and Social
  Security income calculations, which understates them for muni holders. Known, being fixed.
- The three selectable items are conversion approaches. There is no general "choose your objective"
  optimizer.
- It is an educational modeling tool, not financial advice, and copy must never read as a
  recommendation to take a specific action.

## TONE CONSTRAINTS

- No hype adjectives. This audience is skeptical and detail-oriented. Specificity beats enthusiasm.
- Never frame past versions as having been misleading or dishonest. Neutral and forward-looking.
- Do not use the em dash character anywhere. Use commas, colons, or periods.
- Avoid obvious AI-writing tells: mechanical rule-of-three lists, "it's not just X, it's Y," heavy
  hedging, and closing sentences that restate what was just said. Vary sentence rhythm.

## USING THE REAL NUMBERS (cleared for publication)

The figures below come from one household I modeled in the live app. They are real output, not
invented, and I am comfortable publishing them. They must be presented with their context, because
the household is affluent and the numbers are not typical:

**The household:** married filing jointly, born 1955, Florida resident (no state income tax),
about $8.24M in traditional IRA assets, $1.1M in Roth, roughly $300,000 a year of spending, and
about $87,000 a year of Social Security.

**The figures:**
- Heir benefit of the recommended plan: about **$4.4M in future dollars**, which is about
  **$1.0M in today's dollars**. Same plan, same math, two units. The app now leads with the
  second number.
- Survivor scenario: about **$430,000 in additional projected lifetime tax** if one spouse dies
  early in the plan and the survivor files single for the remaining years.

**How to use them honestly:** anchor them to the household, not to the reader. "In one household I
modeled, with $8M in an IRA" is fair. "You could be losing $430,000" is not, and I will not publish
that. The point of the present-value figure specifically is that it is the LESS impressive number
and the app shows it anyway.

## CANDIDATE ANGLES (react to these, and propose better ones)

1. **The widow penalty.** A surviving spouse keeps most of the household income but files single,
   with roughly half the brackets and half the standard deduction. In the modeled household
   described above, the survivor's projected additional lifetime tax was about $430,000. Concrete,
   emotional, badly under-discussed. Note: I have an unsent pitch to a Wall Street Journal reporter
   built on this angle, so tell me whether publishing it on LinkedIn first strengthens or spends it.
2. **Nominal versus present value.** "+$4.4M for your heirs" and "+$1.0M for your heirs" can
   describe the same plan. Most tools show you the flattering one.
3. **The ACA and IRMAA pincer.** Income at 62 to 64 sets both today's ACA premium and the Medicare
   surcharge two years later. Same decision, two bills, years apart.
4. **Inherited IRA rules most calculators get wrong.** Ten years, no annual RMD, or annual RMDs
   plus a year-ten drain, depending on facts most tools never ask about.
5. **Builder story.** Solo developer, 1,900 tests, no venture money, no data harvesting.
