## WHAT SHIPPED IN 2.1.2 (verified against commits)

Released July 18, 2026. Build 62. Approved on both the iOS and Mac App Stores.

**1. Rebuilt how the optimizer values future tax dollars (the big one).**
The engine discounted future tax payments at 3% while the account balances those taxes act on
compounded at 6%. That mismatch mis-priced every future tax flow. Over a 25 year horizon it
inflated the terminal tax penalty about 2.05x, so a 22% future tax rate was effectively scored as
~45%, and a ~35% heir rate as ~72%. The practical consequence: the optimizer thought converting
everything, even at a 37% marginal rate, looked tax-optimal. Now every tax flow discounts at the
growth rate, which makes minimizing the objective mathematically equivalent to maximizing the
after-tax estate. Recommendations became materially less aggressive.

**2. Fixed the heir trade-off curve.**
The app plots a frontier showing the trade between your lifetime taxes and what heirs keep. On some
profiles it plotted dominated points, meaning leaning toward heirs showed MORE tax for you AND
FEWER dollars for them, which is nonsense. Now Pareto-repaired across all weights, so the curve
genuinely opens and leaning toward heirs actually helps heirs.

**3. Per-year expense overrides.**
You can now override spending for any individual year rather than assuming one flat inflated
baseline. Handles both a permanent change from year X forward and one-time spikes (a roof, a car, a
wedding). Includes a behavior-preserving migration so existing plans produce identical results.

**4. Local and city income tax.**
Previously only the SALT deduction existed. Now you can enter a local rate (New York City is about
3.88%) and it applies to the same state-taxable base, including in no-state-income-tax states.

**5. State withholding as a percentage.**
Previously dollars only. Now dollars or percent, matching how the federal fields already worked.

**6. Survivor ("widow penalty") figure now literally true.**
The banner says "the survivor is projected to pay about $X more in lifetime tax." X was previously a
discounted internal objective figure. It now reports the actual nominal tax paid over the horizon.
In one real test household the difference was $283k (old display) vs $430k (actual).

**7. Present value made the default display.**
Multi-year results now default to present value with a toggle to future dollars, so the comparison
table is unit-consistent on first view. In a real test household, a headline "+$4.4M for your heirs"
in nominal dollars was +$1.0M in present value. Same plan, honestly labeled.

**8. Two data-integrity fixes.** Assumption edits now always trigger recalculation, and the Social
Security save path can never erase stored data on a failed load.

**Quality bar:** 1,403 automated engine/UI tests plus 503 additional tests, all green at ship.

## CANDIDATE STORY HOOKS (pick or improve)

1. **The widow penalty.** A surviving spouse keeps most of the household income but files single,
   with roughly half the brackets and half the standard deduction. Concrete, emotional, badly
   under-discussed, and the app now quantifies it honestly.
2. **Nominal vs present value.** "+$4.4M for your heirs" and "+$1.0M for your heirs" can describe
   the same plan. Most tools quietly show you the flattering one. Good honesty-in-numbers angle.
3. **I found a bug that was making my own app too aggressive.** The discounting mismatch made
   converting at 37% look optimal. Builder-credibility angle; risk is it reads as "the app was
   wrong," so handle with care.
4. **The ACA/IRMAA pincer.** Income at 62-64 sets both today's ACA premium and the Medicare
   surcharge two years later. I've written a fact-checked article on this already.
5. **Local tax invisibility.** Most planners ignore city income tax entirely. NYC is ~3.88% and it
   compounds across a multi-year conversion ladder.

## HARD CONSTRAINTS

**Accuracy. Do not claim any of the following, because they are not true:**
- No account linking, aggregation, or auto-import of any kind (except an optional Social Security
  earnings-history XML file).
- No Monte Carlo or probability-of-success simulation. Projections are deterministic.
- Voluntary withdrawals from an inherited IRA are not yet modeled in the multi-year projection.
- Tax-exempt municipal bond interest is currently treated as zero in the IRMAA/ACA/Social Security
  MAGI calculations, which understates them for muni holders. Known gap, being fixed.
- The three selectable items are conversion *approaches*. There is no general "pick your objective"
  optimizer.

**Tone:**
- Never frame changes as fixing dishonesty or as "we were misleading you." Neutral and
  forward-looking: refinements, accuracy improvements, enhanced calculations.
- Never imply personalized financial advice.
- Do not use the em dash character anywhere in the copy. Use commas, colons, or periods.
- Avoid obvious AI-writing tells: mechanical rule-of-three lists, "it's not just X, it's Y,"
  heavy hedging, and summary sentences that restate what was just said. Vary sentence rhythm.
- No hype adjectives. The audience is skeptical and detail-oriented; specificity outperforms
  enthusiasm with them.
