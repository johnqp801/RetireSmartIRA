## Final text (LOCKED)

Last month I shipped an update that made my app's biggest number 77 percent smaller. On purpose.

RetireSmartIRA models retirement tax decisions: Roth conversions, RMDs, Medicare surcharges, and what your heirs keep after tax. For one household I modeled, a Florida couple with about $8 million in a traditional IRA, the recommended plan showed a benefit to heirs of $4.4 million.

That number is real. It is also in future dollars, spread across decades. In today's dollars, the same plan is worth about $1.0 million.

Same plan. Same math. Two different units. Most software leads with the bigger one, because the bigger one sells. I made present value the default instead.

That choice comes down to one thing: whether a tool trusts you with the truth or manages your impression of it.

It is the same reason every number in the app is traceable. Most planners hand you a result and a proprietary score, and when you ask why, the answer is some version of "the optimizer decided." RetireSmartIRA does the opposite. Tap any figure and follow it back to the input that produced it. Disagree with a result, and you can see where it came from, then change it.

The app is free right now on the iPhone, iPad, and Mac App Stores. No account, no ads. Your numbers never leave your device, because there is no server to send them to.

See it or download it here: https://apps.apple.com/app/id6759405282

When you look at a retirement projection, would you rather see future dollars first, or today's?
