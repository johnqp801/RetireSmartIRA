## App Store "What's New" (APPROVED, final)

> **2.3: Choose How You Pay Conversion Tax**
>
> • New: decide how each Roth conversion's tax gets funded. Withhold it from the conversion, pay it from your other accounts, or pay it from money outside the plan. Existing plans keep the behavior they already had.
> • Federal withholding is applied against federal income tax only. State tax, IRMAA, NIIT and ACA amounts are funded separately rather than netted against a federal overpayment.
> • Pennsylvania: the share of a conversion withheld for federal tax is treated as PA-taxable, following PA guidance.
> • New funding check: a year whose tax cannot be covered by your taxable and IRA balances is now marked, with the reason on the row and a count at the top of the plan. This is a new check rather than a change to the math, so some existing plans will start showing it.
> • Under 59½: the 10% early-distribution penalty is not modeled, and a note now says so where it could apply.
>
> **Accuracy improvements**
> • Refined how Social Security becomes taxable at lower provisional income, and a withdrawal taken to pay your tax bill now counts toward that calculation.
> • Medicare IRMAA income now follows Medicare's own definition, which does not add back the untaxed portion of your benefits.
> • Withdrawals taken to cover a tax bill now solve to the dollar, so ending balances reflect everything actually withdrawn.
> • ACA income now includes a withdrawal taken to pay tax.
> • Required withdrawals: spouse-owned and jointly held accounts are attributed correctly, and the projection chart's year labels are easier to read.

**Length:** 1,561 characters, well inside the 4,000-character App Store limit.

## What is actually in this release

**Headline feature (V2.3):** selectable Roth conversion tax-funding mode for multi-year. Honors the written commitment to customer Joan Menard. Federal withholding reconciles against federal liability only; state / IRMAA / NIIT / ACA always fund through the cascade. Roth receives gross minus withholding, with PA DOR Ans 274 treatment of the withheld portion. Infeasible years are marked and propagate an unreliability flag forward.

**Six engine and UX fixes merged after V2.3:**

1. `1f006a0` **Pub 915 50% tier.** IRC §86(a)(1) takes the lesser of one-half of benefits or one-half of the excess over the base amount. The halving on the excess was missing, overstating taxable benefits by up to 2x for single filers with provisional income $25,000-$34,000 and MFJ $32,000-$44,000.
2. `6247f01` **Gross-up convergence.** The tax-funding fixed point stopped after 3 passes, leaving up to $13,894 of a year's tax unfunded on solvent households. Now converges via Aitken extrapolation at no measurable perf cost.
3. `59f8f04` **Social Security recursion.** A withdrawal taken to pay tax is ordinary income and raises provisional income. It was computed once pre-withdrawal and never revisited. Worst case measured: taxable benefits understated by $11,888, tax by $4,809.
4. `c5d3fcc` **Cash-flow tax funding.** Unspent income now counts toward funding a year's tax, so a household whose income covers the bill is no longer flagged. Plus a rewrite of the funding-feasibility copy.
5. `8a8f481` **IRMAA MAGI vs ACA MAGI.** Different statutory quantities (42 U.S.C. §1395r(i)(4) vs IRC §36B(d)(2)(B)) differing by exactly the untaxed half of benefits. IRMAA income was overstated by $20,704 at a $3,000/month benefit. Also repairs an optimizer overshoot into the IRMAA cliff buffer that this fix exposed.
6. `2d89141` **TAXSIM 50%-band oracle coverage.** Internal test coverage only.

**Two RMD fixes** folded in from `root-workspace`: chart x-axis label thinning, and spouse/joint account ownership in RMD math (which had produced a $450,000 phantom RMD).

## MUST NOT claim (carried forward from the V2.3 spec review, still binding)

- That ACA **subsidy or premium dollars** are corrected. Only MAGI and cliff detection are; `taxBreakdownFinal` still passes `acaPremiumImpact` through on the pre-gross-up basis.
- That infeasible strategies are **excluded from comparisons or ranking**. Nothing implements that. A test pins the absence of the claim.
- That the app **reduces conversions** to a fundable amount. It does not; the requested conversion is preserved.
- That **§72(t)** is modeled. It is not. It is disclosed.
- That a warning appears when a plan came from a **newer app version**. That flag is recorded but never surfaced.
- Do not lead with `"external"` scenarios migrating unchanged. Fixed, but not a headline.

The one item from the old must-not list that is now RETIRED: the Roth/HSA-only banner. That was genuinely fixed in `c5d3fcc`, so the feasibility warning no longer tells a solvent household it could not reach its own plan.

## Deliberately left OUT of user-facing copy

- **TAXSIM oracle work.** Internal test fixtures. Invisible to users.
- **Funding-feasibility copy rewrite.** It changes wording on a warning most users never see, and calling it out invites the question of what the old wording said. The change itself is real and worth having; it just does not belong in What's New.

## The two options NOT chosen (kept for the record)

### Option B: Accuracy-led

> **2.3: Sharper Numbers, and a Choice About Conversion Tax**
>
> **Refined calculations**
> • Social Security taxation is refined at lower provisional income, where only part of a benefit becomes taxable.
> • A withdrawal taken to pay your tax bill now feeds back into Social Security taxation and into ACA income, as it does in practice.
> • Medicare IRMAA income now follows Medicare's definition and leaves out the untaxed portion of your benefits.
> • Tax-funding withdrawals solve to the dollar, so ending balances reflect the full amount withdrawn.
> • Required withdrawals now attribute spouse-owned and jointly held accounts correctly.
>
> **New: choose how conversion tax is paid**
> Withhold from the conversion, pay from your other accounts, or pay from outside money. Existing plans are unchanged. Federal withholding covers federal income tax only; state tax, IRMAA, NIIT and ACA fund separately. In Pennsylvania the withheld share of a conversion is PA-taxable under PA guidance.
>
> **New funding check**
> Years whose tax your taxable and IRA balances cannot cover are now marked, with a reason and a plan-level count. It is a check, not a math change, so some existing plans will begin showing it. The 10% penalty under 59½ is still not modeled, and is now disclosed.

### Option C: Concise

> **2.3: Conversion Tax, Your Way**
>
> • Choose how each Roth conversion's tax is funded: withheld from the conversion, paid from your other accounts, or paid from outside money. Existing plans are unchanged.
> • Withholding applies to federal income tax only. State tax, IRMAA, NIIT and ACA fund separately. Pennsylvania treats the withheld share as PA-taxable.
> • Years your balances cannot fund are now flagged with a reason. This is a new check, not a change to the math.
> • Refined Social Security taxation at lower incomes, and a tax-funding withdrawal now counts toward it.
> • Medicare IRMAA income now matches Medicare's definition for benefit recipients.
> • Required withdrawals handle spouse-owned and joint accounts correctly.
> • The 10% penalty under 59½ is not modeled, and is now noted where it applies.

**Why A won:** it leads with the feature promised to a customer, and keeps the accuracy items in their own section where they read as refinement rather than repair. B would have been right only if the correctness work should carry the release. C fits the smallest screens but drops the withholding and feasibility nuances, which are the two most likely to generate support questions.
