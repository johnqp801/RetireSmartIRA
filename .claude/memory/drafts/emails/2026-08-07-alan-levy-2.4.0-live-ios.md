## FINAL TEXT (John's, 2026-08-07)

Alan,

2.4.0 is live on the App Store as of today, so it should reach your iPhone shortly if it has not
already.

The cursor fix you requested in July is finally in. Tap into a field that already holds a number and
the cursor now lands after the digits instead of in front of them, so your first keystroke appends
rather than inserting at the front. It applies everywhere numbers are pre-filled, not just on the
Settings screen where you first hit it.

Your NYC pension is fixed in this build as well. New York excludes state and local government pensions
in full on IT-201 line 26, with no $20,000 cap, and because the city tax runs on the state-taxable
amount, the exclusion carries through to the city tax too. The app was previously capping it at
$20,000 and taxing the rest at both.

It needs one selection from you. Open that pension, either where you entered the income or on the
account, and you will see a new row for the plan type. Choose "Government pension, New York State or
local". That is what tells the app it is line 26 income. Your state and city tax should both drop once
it is set, on the single-year screen and in the Multi-Year plan.

On the 457 question you asked a couple of weeks ago: keep using Traditional 401(k) for the account
type, since there is still no 457 option. But the same plan-type row now has "403(b) or 457,
government employer", which identifies it as itself. It is deliberately kept out of the line 26
exclusion, which is correct. Line 26 covers the pension, not a salary-reduction plan.

One thing I have not done. Entering your own future income year by year is still not built. It's
important and on my list but I could not get to it in this latest release cycle.

Thanks for continuing to work with me to make RetireSmartIRA better and more useful to more users!
The pension report in particular turned into a good deal more than a one-state fix.

John

## What John changed, and why it is better

**The standalone apology paragraph is gone.** The machine draft spent four sentences on the fact that
the fix was promised for the release before this one and missed it. John's version carries the same
acknowledgment in one word: *"The cursor fix you requested in July is **finally** in."* It credits him
for asking, dates the ask, and concedes the delay, without making him read a paragraph about a
mistake he already lived through. **Precedent for future tester notes: acknowledge a miss in the
sentence that delivers the fix, rather than in a paragraph of its own.**

**The per-year income line is warmer and still commits to nothing.** *"It's important and on my list
but I could not get to it in this latest release cycle"* names it as real work without setting a date.
That matters because the item was told to Alan as "next on my list" on 2026-07-18 and has now missed
two releases; a third date would have been the third promise.

**The close thanks him as a collaborator rather than a reporter**, which fits an iPhone evangelist who
has now filed three rounds of feedback and one genuine calculation bug.
