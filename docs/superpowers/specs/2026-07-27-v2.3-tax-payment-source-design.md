# V2.3 — Selectable Roth Conversion Tax-Funding Mode (Multi-Year)

**Date:** 2026-07-27 (revision 4, after third design review)
**Status:** Approved design, ready for implementation plan
**Driver:** Written commitment to customer Joan Menard (2026-07-26), targeting early August 2026. See `.claude/memory/decisions/log.md` (2026-07-26 entry) and the user auto-memory `v2-3-tax-payment-source-commitment.md`.

## Background

Joan Menard (found the app via the HumbleDollar "Widow Tax" article) asked whether the multi-year engine correctly handles funding a Roth conversion's tax bill entirely from IRA withdrawals when there's no outside cash, a scenario she was worried about after reading Ed Slott's warning against exactly that. A code audit found the engine already does this correctly by default (`TaxPaymentSource.taxableThenGrossUp`, a real fixed-point gross-up solve in `ProjectionEngine.swift`). What's missing is a user-facing **choice**, the behavior is hardcoded, not selectable. John committed in writing to shipping a selectable tax-funding source in V2.3, early August 2026.

A second audit finding, independent of Joan's question: single-year's `RothConversionWithholdingMode` and multi-year's `TaxPaymentSource` look like parallel settings but govern different mechanics. Single-year controls whether the custodian withholds a slice of the conversion itself; multi-year controls funding *priority* (taxable brokerage first, then IRA gross-up) vs. an untracked "external" assumption. This design brings multi-year the withholding mechanic single-year already has, while keeping multi-year's existing funding-priority behavior.

## The central distinction: remittance vs. funding

**Withholding is a payment toward a tax liability, not a definition of it.** The engine computes what a conversion actually costs; a withholding election only prepays some portion of that. Convert $100,000 at 22% withholding and the custodian remits $22,000, but the tax that conversion actually generates might be $17,000 or $31,000 depending on the household's full picture.

The model tracks **two orthogonal concepts internally**, even though the UI presents three friendly presets:

**Remittance method** — how tax reaches the IRS: withheld by the custodian from the distribution, or paid separately by the user.

**Shortfall-funding policy** — what actually funds the liability (and any balance due): taxable brokerage, an additionally grossed-up IRA withdrawal, or untracked outside money.

### Preset → internal mapping (explicit)

The three-case enum is a set of **user-facing strategy presets**, not a complete domain representation. Each resolves internally into a (remittance method, shortfall-funding policy) pair:

| Preset | Remittance | Shortfall funding |
|---|---|---|
| `.withheldFromConversion` | Custodian withholds at the elected federal rate | Taxable assets first, then iteratively grossed-up IRA withdrawal |
| `.fundedFromAccounts` | Paid separately | Taxable assets first, then iteratively grossed-up IRA withdrawal |
| `.paidFromOutsideMoney` | Paid separately | Untracked external source (no balance modeled) |

Writing the mapping down makes the mechanics unambiguous and makes a future split into two independent user-facing controls straightforward.

### Which liabilities the funding mechanism covers (verified)

The cascade funds the household's **entire annual tax liability**, not merely the incremental tax attributable to the conversion. Verified at `ProjectionEngine.swift:858-859`: `nonFedState = taxBreakdown.total − federalTax − stateTax` (IRMAA + ACA premium impact + NIIT), and `baseTotalTax = federalTax + stateTax + nonFedState`. All five components are funded from accounts.

This is a material distinction — funding only the conversion-incremental slice would withdraw far less — and it is the existing, intended behavior. V2.3 preserves it. Withholding therefore offsets one component of a larger bill, never the bill itself.

### Reconciliation

**Federal withholding is reconciled only against modeled federal income-tax liability.** Withholding is a federal instrument (see below); it does not pay state tax, IRMAA, NIIT, or ACA repayment.

- **Federal balance due (federal withholding < federal liability).** The federal shortfall enters the shortfall-funding cascade.
- **Federal overpayment (federal withholding > federal liability).** The excess is credited to the taxable account as a federal overpayment, after annual growth has been applied.
- **State income tax, IRMAA, NIIT, and ACA impact are calculated and funded separately** through the selected shortfall-funding policy.
- **A federal overpayment is never netted against state or other liability before the fixed-point gross-up is calculated.**

That last rule is not a stylistic preference. Consider federal tax $17,000, federal withholding $22,000, state tax $8,000. Netting first yields a $3,000 balance due. Correct handling yields a $5,000 federal overpayment credited to taxable **and** an $8,000 state liability funded through the cascade. Those differ: if the state liability requires an IRA withdrawal, that withdrawal generates additional federal and state tax, so premature netting seeds the fixed point with the wrong number and converges on an incorrect gross-up.

**The cascade itself.** Whatever enters it is funded from taxable assets first, then, if necessary, through an **iteratively grossed-up IRA withdrawal**, using the existing fixed-point solver (`incrementalTax`, `ProjectionEngine.swift:865`). An additional IRA withdrawal is itself taxable income: the withdrawal raises the tax, which raises the required withdrawal. **The amount needed is not the plain arithmetic difference** — that is only the seed value for the iteration.

**Refund timing is already safe structurally:** growth is applied in Step 5 (`:568`), while the tax debit/funding block is Step 7 (`:857`), so an overpayment credited there lands *after* that year's growth and cannot earn a phantom full year of return. Crediting it in the same projection year remains a deliberate timing simplification (a real refund arrives the following April), documented as such; dropping the excess instead would silently destroy user assets.

### Federal vs. state (verified convention)

Single-year's rate is **federal-only**: `rothConversionFederalWithholdingRate` (`ScenarioStateManager.swift:48`, default 0.24), and `scenarioRothConversionWithholdingAmount` is `gross × that rate`. Multi-year adopts the same convention: **the elected withholding rate applies against federal liability only.**

This matters most in Pennsylvania, where the withholding election can itself increase state liability — and that added state tax is funded by the cascade, never by the withholding that caused it.

### Under-59½ additional tax: NOT modeled (verified limitation)

**Verified across the entire codebase: the §72(t) 10% additional tax on early distributions is not modeled anywhere.** No engine computes it. The 59½ references in `TaxCalculationEngine.swift` (`:519`, `:563`) and `DataManager.swift` (`:747`, `:772`) are **state retirement-income exemption gates** — an unrelated mechanism that determines state exclusion eligibility, not a federal penalty. Single-year's existing warning states the gap plainly: *"This app does not yet model that penalty"* (`RothConversionWithholdingCard.swift:128`).

The consequence compounds and must be stated rather than left implicit. For a user under 59½, both:

1. the **computed liability** is understated (the 10% is missing), and
2. the **required gross-up** is understated (the missing 10% would itself need funding, and an IRA withdrawal taken to fund tax is itself an early distribution, recursively subject to it).

**V2.3 decision: the additional tax remains unmodeled and is disclosed, not silently omitted.** Multi-year must carry the same under-59½ warning single-year already shows, worded to cover both triggers — withholding taken from a conversion, and any grossed-up IRA withdrawal funding a balance due. Modeling §72(t) properly (including the exception matrix under §72(t)(2)) is out of scope for this release and belongs on the backlog.

## Conversion amount is GROSS (verified)

`DataManager.scenarioTotalRothConversion` (`:1420`) is the **gross distribution**. Withholding is computed *from* it (`:1440`), and `scenarioRothConversionNetAmount` (`:1448`) is gross minus withheld. For $100,000 at 22%: IRA decreases $100,000, Roth receives $78,000, $22,000 is remitted, **taxable conversion income is $100,000**.

The withheld amount is **already inside** that $100,000 and must **not** be added to income again. (An earlier draft said the withheld amount "is added to that year's ordinary taxable income," which would have double-counted it.)

**Pennsylvania.** The federal rule is unchanged: the gross conversion is included once, in full. Separately, for Pennsylvania purposes, the portion withheld rather than deposited into the Roth may receive different state-tax treatment from the portion actually converted (PA DOR Ans 274). Reuse the existing single-year `applyRetirementExemptions` logic; do not re-derive it, and do not let it alter the federal inclusion.

No gross-up-to-target-deposit calculation is needed anywhere, since users enter the gross conversion, not a desired net deposit.

## Goals

1. Ship a user-facing choice for how multi-year Roth conversion tax is funded, honoring the Joan Menard commitment.
2. Give multi-year the withholding mechanic single-year already has (including the PA treatment), preserving multi-year's existing funding-priority behavior.
3. Define reconciliation explicitly — including federal/state separation and its recursive shortfall path — so withholding and computed liability can never silently diverge.
4. Make every mode's shortfall policy visible to the user rather than hidden behind a friendly label.
5. Stop presenting unfundable plans as valid recommendations.
6. Preserve saved-scenario behavior across the enum migration.

## Non-goals

- Per-year overrides of the funding mode (one global setting, matching today's `MultiYearAssumptions.taxPaymentSource`).
- Changes to single-year's existing `RothConversionWithholdingCard` behavior (already correct).
- The `taxableSS` pre-gross-up fix — **deliberately deferred, see below**.
- The intra-year IRMAA/ACA-tier-crossing approximation (`ProjectionEngine.swift:952-958`).
- Cross-year modeling of refunds as receivables.
- Splitting remittance and shortfall-funding into two independent user-facing controls (the internal mapping above makes this a later, non-breaking change).
- Modeling the §72(t) 10% additional tax (disclosed as an existing limitation, see above).
- Automatic conversion-reduction to reach a fundable amount (requires joint convergence of conversion and funding; see Infeasible years).

## Scope for V2.3

1. Withholding mode + the reconciliation rules above, with federal/state separation; all modes exposed via one 3-way picker with explicit funding-order labels.
2. Infeasible-year marking: shortfall recorded, dependent years flagged unreliable, strategy excluded from unqualified lifetime comparisons.
3. Migration layer with explicit raw-value mapping and unknown-value handling.
4. Ed Slott disclosure copy, plus the under-59½ §72(t) limitation disclosure in multi-year.
5. `underfunded` surfaced with amount, cause, and consequence (see UI).
6. ACA MAGI fix (`ProjectionEngine.swift:681`), mirroring the IRMAA "A3 fix" (`:943-959`).
7. Move the withholding-rate list out of the view layer into a shared domain type.

**Minimum shippable feature**, if the schedule tightens: migration, the picker, reconciliation (including federal/state separation), infeasible-year marking, accurate labels, both disclosures, and basic `underfunded` visibility. Cut order is item 6 (the independent ACA MAGI correction) first, then enhanced CPA-briefing presentation beyond a basic warning, then non-essential UI polish. **Do not cut either disclosure** — this feature exists because a customer was worried about using IRA assets to pay conversion tax; shipping it without that disclosure would be backwards. **Do not cut infeasible-year marking** — without it the release surfaces a shortfall number while still presenting the plan as valid, which is the defect it is meant to fix.

## Deferred to 2.3.1 / 2.4

**The `taxableSS` frozen-pre-gross-up fix (`ProjectionEngine.swift:626`).** The recursion is real and the fix is correct: a gross-up withdrawal raises ordinary income → can raise taxable Social Security → raises tax → raises the required gross-up → can raise taxable SS again. Recalculating taxable SS inside the fixed-point solver is the right answer.

Deferred because it is the only change here touching the shared solver **every existing projection depends on**. A subtle error would move numbers in plans that never touch the new picker. Ships separately with dedicated regression testing and release notes explaining why some projections legitimately changed.

## Infeasible years: what actually happens when funding runs out

**Current behavior (verified, and it is the bug):** `underfundedTax` is computed at `ProjectionEngine.swift:939` and stored at `:1053`. Nothing else happens. The projection continues normally with part of the tax bill simply unpaid, and no code path reduces the conversion for fundability reasons — the clamping at `:264-299` limits conversions against available *balance* and RMD reservation, not against ability to pay the resulting tax. The engine therefore already presents economically impossible plans as valid recommendations. Surfacing this is the highest-severity item in the release.

Three behaviors were available. V2.3 chooses the third:

1. *Reduce the conversion until the tax becomes fundable* — requires recalculating conversion, liability, and funding requirement **together** to a converged fundable amount. The engine cannot do this today; the existing fixed point solves for the withdrawal given a fixed conversion, not for both jointly. Building it is real optimizer work, not in scope here.
2. *Keep the conversion and carry an unpaid liability* — today's behavior. Produces an economically impossible plan and is what this release exists to stop.
3. **Mark the year infeasible.** Chosen.

**Specified behavior.** If available taxable and traditional IRA assets cannot fund the converged tax requirement, the year is marked **infeasible**. The engine records the precise funding shortfall and preserves the requested conversion for diagnostic display, but does not present the plan as a fully funded recommendation. All subsequent years that depend on the infeasible balance state are marked **unreliable**, and the strategy is **excluded from unqualified lifetime comparisons** (it must not silently win a lifetime-tax ranking by declining to pay part of its tax bill). V2.3 does **not** automatically reduce the requested conversion, because doing so correctly requires the joint convergence described in option 1.

Automatic conversion-reduction is a candidate for a later release, once the engine can solve conversion and funding together.

## Output-stability promise (scoped precisely)

1. **The enum migration itself must not alter any scenario's funding behavior.** A legacy value must resolve to exactly the behavior it had before.
2. **Existing scenarios left untouched preserve prior projections, except where the ACA MAGI correction intentionally changes results.** That exception is not limited to ACA-labeled figures: a corrected ACA MAGI can change the subsidy, which changes the ACA premium impact inside `taxBreakdown.total`, which changes the amount the cascade must fund, which changes the gross-up withdrawal, which changes end-of-year balances and therefore every subsequent year. The stability promise must accommodate those **downstream effects**, not merely the ACA line item.
3. Infeasible-year marking changes how affected plans are *presented and compared*, though not the underlying arithmetic of feasible years.
4. Broader movement (notably from the deferred `taxableSS` fix) belongs to its own release and its own release notes.

## Data model

Replace the 2-case `TaxPaymentSource` (`MultiYearAssumptions.swift:10-13`) with:

```swift
enum RothTaxFundingMode: String, Codable, Sendable {
    case withheldFromConversion   // custodian withholds at elected federal rate; shortfall → taxable, then IRA
    case fundedFromAccounts       // paid separately; taxable assets first, then grossed-up IRA withdrawal
    case paidFromOutsideMoney     // paid separately from an untracked external source
}
```

Plus a federal withholding-rate field, used only in `.withheldFromConversion`.

**Withholding-rate list ownership.** The seven supported rates (10/12/22/24/32/35/37%) currently live as a `private let` inside `RothConversionWithholdingCard.swift:47-50` — a domain rule owned by a SwiftUI view. Move them to a shared model/tax-domain type consumed by both single-year and multi-year UI, so the multi-year engine never depends on a view and the two screens cannot drift.

**Migration.** `MultiYearAssumptions.swift:103` currently decodes with `decodeIfPresent(...) ?? .taxableThenGrossUp`. Under a renamed enum, old raw strings would fail to match and **silently fall back to the default** — a silent behavior change on saved plans, worse than a loud failure. The decoder must distinguish four cases:

| Stored value | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `"taxableThenGrossUp"` (known legacy) | → `.fundedFromAccounts` |
| `"external"` (known legacy) | → `.paidFromOutsideMoney` |
| Known current value | Decode normally |
| Field absent | → `.fundedFromAccounts` (existing default; safe) |
| Unknown / corrupt string | **Recoverable migration warning or decoding error — never a silent default.** |

The unknown case matters because a scenario written by a future version could otherwise be opened by an older build and quietly reinterpreted as a different funding strategy.

Tested against **real archived scenario files**, not just round-tripped fresh objects.

## Engine changes (`ProjectionEngine.swift`)

1. **Withholding mode + reconciliation** — new branch alongside the existing funding block at `:857`. Net Roth deposit = gross × (1 − federal rate); gross conversion income unchanged; PA treatment reused from single-year. Then apply the reconciliation rules: federal withholding offsets **federal liability only**; the federal shortfall (if any) plus state, IRMAA, NIIT, and ACA impact enter the cascade **un-netted**, seeding the **existing fixed-point solver** (not a plain subtraction); a federal overpayment credits the taxable bucket in Step 7 (post-growth).
2. **Infeasible-year marking** — when the converged requirement exceeds available taxable + traditional assets, mark the year infeasible and propagate an unreliable flag to dependent later years. `underfundedTax` (`:939`) already computes the amount; this adds the state and its propagation, plus exclusion from unqualified lifetime comparisons.
3. **ACA MAGI fix (`:681`)** — `acaMagiValue` computes from pre-gross-up `federalAGI + magiAddback` and is never updated, unlike `irmaaMagi`, which correctly moved to post-gross-up `reportedAGI + magiAddback` in the existing A3 fix. Apply the same substitution before it's stored at `:1044`. Note the downstream chain in Output-stability item 2.
4. **`underfunded` reporting** — the value already exists on `YearRecommendation`; this release makes it explanatory (see UI).

## UI

New card modeled on `RothConversionWithholdingCard.swift`'s pattern (segmented picker + conditional rate sub-picker + PA note + under-59½ warning), in the Multi-Year Assumptions sheet.

**Labels must state the full behavior, including shortfall policy.** A friendly label that hides a funding cascade is the main usability risk in this design:

- **"Withhold from conversion"** → subtitle: *"Withhold at the selected federal rate; use taxable assets, then additional IRA withdrawals, for any remaining tax."* Without this, a user reasonably assumes withholding is the only modeled payment and that any balance is handled outside the plan.
- **"Pay from my accounts"** → subtitle: *"Taxable assets first, then IRA if needed."* This matters acutely for Joan's own profile: with no taxable account she lands in IRA-gross-up by default rather than by choice.
- **"Pay from outside money"** → subtitle noting no balance is tracked.

**`underfunded` must explain, not just flag**, and now has definite answers to give. Surface the amount, the cause, and the consequence — e.g. *"Tax funding shortfall: $8,420. Available taxable and IRA assets were insufficient to fund the modeled tax for this year."* Per the Infeasible years section, the in-app and CPA-briefing copy states plainly: the requested conversion was **not** reduced (it is shown as requested, for diagnosis); the year is marked **infeasible**; later years that depend on its balances are **unreliable**; and the strategy is **excluded from unqualified lifetime comparisons**.

**Two disclosures, distinct triggers:**
- **Ed Slott (IRA dollars paying conversion tax)** appears on BOTH `.withheldFromConversion` and `.fundedFromAccounts`, since IRA dollars pay the tax in both: withholding takes it straight out of the distribution, and the gross-up pulls an extra withdrawal.
- **Under-59½ §72(t) limitation** appears whenever either spouse is under 59½ and the selected mode can touch IRA dollars, matching single-year's existing warning but worded to cover both triggers (withholding from the conversion, and a grossed-up withdrawal funding a balance due). It must say the additional tax is **not modeled**, so the projection understates both the liability and the required withdrawal.

Copy added to `V2Disclosures.swift`.

## Testing

Full suite (1,400+ Swift Testing / 500+ XCTest) green after each change, plus business-rule tests that assert intent rather than pinning current output:

- Migration: both known legacy values, known current values, absent field, and an **unknown value producing a warning/error rather than a silent default** — against real archived scenario files
- Gross vs. net accounting (income = gross, not gross + withheld)
- Under-withholding: federal balance due converges through the fixed-point solver, **not** a plain arithmetic difference
- Over-withholding: federal excess credited to taxable, no dollars destroyed, no full-year growth applied to the credit
- **Federal overpayment coexisting with state liability** — the $17k/$22k/$8k case from Reconciliation: asserts a $5,000 credit and an $8,000 cascade-funded liability, **not** a $3,000 net, and asserts the gross-up is seeded from the un-netted state figure
- Funding covers the **whole** annual liability (federal + state + IRMAA + ACA + NIIT), not the conversion-incremental slice
- Pennsylvania: withheld-vs-converted portion state treatment, with federal gross inclusion unchanged; the PA tax created by withholding is cascade-funded
- Under-59½: the §72(t) disclosure appears; the projection is asserted to **exclude** the additional tax (pins the documented limitation so a future fix is a deliberate, visible change)
- **Infeasible years:** shortfall recorded, conversion NOT reduced, year marked infeasible, dependent later years marked unreliable, strategy excluded from unqualified lifetime comparisons
- ACA MAGI after gross-up; IRMAA after gross-up; plus the ACA **downstream** chain (subsidy → `taxBreakdown.total` → cascade amount → gross-up → later balances)
- Gross-up crossing Social Security taxation thresholds (documents current pre-fix behavior; becomes the regression baseline for the deferred `taxableSS` work)
- IRA depletion (`availableTrad` clamp, `:890`) and taxable depletion → `underfunded` (`:939`) — existing but currently unasserted paths

## Risks

- **Timeline.** Deferring `taxableSS` materially reduces schedule and debugging risk. Cut order is defined above; neither disclosure nor infeasible-year marking is a cut candidate.
- **Reconciliation introduces new semantics.** The same-year federal-overpayment credit is a modeling decision, not tax law. Disclose in-app wherever over-withholding materially affects a projection.
- **Infeasible-year marking will make some existing plans stop looking valid.** That is the intended correction — those plans were never fundable — but it is a visible change in what the app tells users, and needs release-note language framing it as a feasibility check rather than a calculation change.
- **Under-59½ users receive knowingly understated projections.** Mitigated by disclosure only. If this segment turns out to be material, §72(t) modeling should be prioritized, since the understatement compounds through the gross-up.
- **Preset abstraction.** The 3-case enum deliberately collapses two orthogonal concepts. Acceptable for V2.3 given the explicit mapping table above; revisit if users need combinations the presets don't express.
- **Terminology.** The new enum's case names deliberately differ from single-year's `RothConversionWithholdingMode`, since multi-year models a third option. User-facing labels should read consistently across both screens even though the underlying types differ.
